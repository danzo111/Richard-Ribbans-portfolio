Selected projects

MSc research · University of Cape Town · 2024 – 2025

A 3D model of the Fimbul Ice Shelf, inside and out

Ice shelves crack from underneath, where nobody can see. Walking a radar across crevassed ice is slow and dangerous, and satellites are too coarse to show the shape of the base. I flew the radar on a drone instead.

80 – 89% of the ice shelf’s thickness is already cut through by basal crevasses at its western calving face. The model puts a number on a risk that was last mapped in 2011.

How I did it

  1. Planned the campaignChose drone-borne radar after a scoping review of how ice shelves are surveyed, then mapped the working area, transects and supply route.
  2. Flew the lines28 radar flights as pilot in command, 5 m above the surface at 4 m/s, with GNSS control from a Trimble R4S.
  3. Cleaned the radargramsTested noise-reduction methods and showed BM3D filtering removes noise without losing the fine layering.
  4. Wrote new extraction methodsWavelet and Hough transforms couldn’t handle layering this dense, so I developed two of my own: one for horizontal layers, one for basal crevasse tips.
  5. Built the modelConverted the extracted features into georeferenced point clouds and assembled the 3D geometry of the shelf.
